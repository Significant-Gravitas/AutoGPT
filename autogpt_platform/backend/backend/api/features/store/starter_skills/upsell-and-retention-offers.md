# Upsell and retention offers

Use this when a support touch can attach value or a churn signal appears.
Offer in-flow, run the save play, and never discount past your limit.

## What you need first

The case or signal, the customer's plan and usage, the offer sheet and
discount limit from prefs, and the save-play ladder.

## Signal, match, offer, save

1. Read the signal before pitching: upgrade asked, usage capped, competitor
   named, cancellation started, or executive sponsor gone quiet. No signal
   means no pitch — solve the case and stop.
2. Match one offer to the signal: the plan that removes the cap, the add-on
   that fixes the complaint, the annual switch that answers the price gripe.
   Quote real prices and terms from the offer sheet; a term you cannot
   source is UNKNOWN, never filled in.
3. Draft the in-flow offer inside the fix, not instead of it: solve first,
   then one plain paragraph with the offer, the price, and the easy yes.
   Discounts, credits, and exceptions stay inside the approval limit —
   anything past it gets staged for owner yes, never promised.
4. For churn in motion, run the save ladder one rung at a time: understand,
   fix, offer, escalate to the save owner. If renewal itself is at risk,
   hand the save plan to Account health and QBRs. Log each rung with the
   customer's words. A lost save gets a graceful close draft, not pressure.
5. Log offer, signal, verdict, and revenue touch. Offer to flag repeat save
   reasons as feedback themes: route each theme to its owner, follow up
   with the reporter on what changed, triage complaints separately from
   general feedback — and never promise to pass it on without doing it.

## Output

The matched offer draft or save-ladder plan with its approval state, and
the case log line.

## When the records are thin

No offer sheet in prefs means you solve the case and name the missing
sheet — never invent prices, plans, or discount authority.
