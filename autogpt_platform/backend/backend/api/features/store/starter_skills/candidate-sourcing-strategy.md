# Candidate sourcing strategy

Run this when the user asks for candidates, wants more names for the open
role, names a benchmark person to clone, or asks how hard the search will be.
This is also the pass behind an on-demand sourcing batch.

## Inputs

The role scorecard, the shortlist so far, the pipeline list, and how many
names they want — default 10. Set the goal up front: names wanted plus a
reply-rate target, default 20 percent in 30 days, with the channels and who
owns each.

## Dedupe before you search

Read the shortlist and the pipeline list first so nobody is surfaced twice.
Dedupe on name plus current company, normalised for case, accents,
punctuation, and nicknames. Near matches go in a short "check these" list,
never merged on a hunch. Anyone marked do not contact stays out of everything.
Before any cold search, re-engage the near-misses from the last 12 to 18
months; the warm pool converts first.

## Work the sources you can actually read

Company team and about pages, engineering and design blog bylines, changelog
and release-note authors, GitHub profiles and organisation members, package
and repository maintainers, public design files and case studies, conference
and meetup speaker lists, podcast guest pages, portfolios and personal sites,
public writing, and public professional profiles on the open web. Say which
ones you used. A site that blocks you gets named, not guessed around.

When the user names a benchmark person, search the nearby graph first: people
who worked on the same product in the same years, co-speakers and co-authors,
co-maintainers, people they cite and people who cite them. Then search the
pattern more widely at peer companies. Say plainly which part of the benchmark
you could not match.

## Post progress while you work

Names on screen inside a minute beats a perfect batch in ten.

## One card per person

Name, current title and company, where they are based, two to four evidence
lines that each map to a must-have with the link that proves it, the tenure
pattern, anything they have said publicly about what they want next, and one
line on the gap or the risk.

Every card carries at least one source link. No link, no card. Never list a
person you cannot point to, and never write that someone is open to a move
unless they said so in public. Use only what the user gave you and what the
person published about their own work. Never record or guess age, gender,
race, nationality, religion, health, or family status, and never read anything
off a photo.

## Rank, save, and track

Rank the batch into strong fit, worth a look, and stretch, with one line of
reasoning each. Add every new name to the shortlist with the date and the
source. Track conversion per stage — contacted, replied, screened, in loop —
so the next batch knows which channel earned it.

## The market read

When they ask how hard the search is: who else is hiring the same person right
now with links, where the people cluster with counts from your own batches,
pay only from postings that state a number with links, which must-have is
costing the most, and one recommendation on the single change that helps most.
Say up front that it is a read off public information and a small sample, not
a market report.

## Output

The ranked batch as cards in chat, saved to the shortlist, plus the market
read when asked. Triage runs five at a time on request: keep, pass, or maybe
with the reason in a few words on every pass. Three passes for the same reason
means the bar is wrong, so propose the exact scorecard edit and apply it only
on a yes.

## Fallbacks

A thin batch gets reported as thin, with what is blocking it. Never pad a
batch to hit the number. Too few batches for a real market read gets one line
saying so, and the read after the next one.

## Approval gate

Sourcing only. Nothing here contacts anyone: no message, no connection
request, no note goes out without the owner's explicit yes for that specific
message.
