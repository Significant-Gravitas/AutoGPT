# Competitive intelligence

Use this when the user names a competitor, a battlecard, or win-loss, or
asks how you win against someone. Start from the competitors, what your
product sells, and the decision the read informs. Gong call excerpts and
win-loss notes, where they exist, are the quotes the win strategy rests
on.

## Set the tiered watch list first

Tier 1 direct competitors for a deep read, Tier 2 adjacent substitutes for
a skim, Tier 3 aspirational for a monthly read. Each gets its pricing,
changelog, product, and careers pages logged — including fetches that fail
— plus review-site mining, competitor demo notes, and community chatter
where they exist.

## Read what changed

Group the changes by competitor and cite the link and date on each line.
Keep to moves that matter: launches, pricing, messaging, positioning shifts.
A competitor with nothing worth reporting is left out entirely, and an
unlinked claim never makes the page.

## Write the so-what

Two to four lines per material move, for this user's GTM job. A launch
gets a positioning read, a pricing move gets a packaging read, a content
push gets an enablement read. Say it is unclear when it is unclear.

## Turn the read into win strategy

Three discriminators with proof, the competitor line the rep will hear,
and the counter in rep register. Fold in win-loss quotes where they
exist, quoted not paraphrased.

## Build or refresh the battlecard

One page per competitor, scannable in under two minutes. Each card
carries win/loss zones (where you win, where you battle, where you lose),
the Know/Say/Show for the rep's exact words, scenario variants for
head-to-head versus rip-and-replace (the pain of staying must outweigh
the pain of switching), and a 90-day deal-intel pane with the latest win,
loss, and at-risk quotes. Refresh on signal, never on schedule alone —
changelog, pricing, review batches, exec changes, and earnings trigger a
same-week versioned diff — with a biweekly floor for Tier 1 cards. Every
card names its owner, last refresh, and approver; a card with no owner is
retired until it has one.

## What you hand back

The brief, saved dated with sources and a full source ledger including
failures. A quiet market is one line saying so, not a brief. Never brief
the same change twice — fold in what the routine already logged.

## Fallbacks

No competitor list means you draft the watch list from the market and ask
for a yes. No pricing pages means you say so and refuse the pricing read.
Never pad the brief to look busy, and never invent a competitor move, a
price, or a customer. Battlecards publish rep-wide only on the owner's
explicit yes.
