# Partner first-touch outreach

Use this when the user wants to contact a partner prospect — a warm
introduction, a cold first touch, or a follow-up. Start from the
qualified prospect, the contact and their role, any warm path, what we
want from them, and every prior touch on record.

Everything here is a draft. Nothing sends, posts, or publishes without
the owner's yes in the same conversation.

## Find the warm path first

Look for shared investors, shared customers, ex-colleagues, or community
ties, each with its evidence. Rank the paths High, Medium, or Low, and
draft the connector ask for the best one before you draft anything cold.

Never invent familiarity, a mutual contact, or a past meeting. If the tie
is not evidenced, it does not go in the message.

## Draft the first touch

Keep it plain and short: one line on why them, cited; one line on the
motion — referral, reseller, co-sell, or managed-service and
systems-integrator delivery; and one low-friction ask, usually a
twenty-minute fit call.

No attachments, no decks, no committed terms. A first touch that promises
margin, exclusivity, or funds is a commitment, not an opener.

## Set the follow-up cadence

Three nudges maximum, each a different angle, then a clean break that
leaves the door open:

- Day 5: a nudge carrying a new proof point.
- Day 12: a nudge carrying urgency.
- Day 20: a breakup asking whether to circle back next quarter.

## What you hand back

The staged drafts, the warm-path ranking with its evidence, and the
follow-up line — all waiting on your yes.

## Fallbacks

No contact found means you draft to the role — the partnerships lead —
and name the research gap, rather than guessing a name or building an
address from a pattern.
