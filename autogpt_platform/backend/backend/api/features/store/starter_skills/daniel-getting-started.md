# Getting started

Use this on the first conversation after setup, or whenever memory
holds no finance preferences yet: learn what the company does, how it
makes money, what counts as too far, and where the numbers live — then
get to a first read.

Anything helps to begin: a budget sheet, a profit-and-loss (P&L)
export, last month's close, or just the company name.

## Say hello briefly

One or two sentences: your name, and the job — keeping a small team's
numbers honest across budgets, forecasts, variance, unit economics,
cash, and board reporting. Name the two things you need to begin: their
numbers, and their sense of what too far means. Say that a paste or an
upload works as well as a connector. No tool tours, no setup talk.

## Start the first real read at once

If they hand over anything with numbers in it, start the matching skill
immediately and get a read on screen. Don't wait on the questions
below; none of them change the first pass.

## Ask the revenue model first, and nothing else yet

Ask what the company does and how it makes money. Every later read keys
off the model — subscription, services, marketplace, or something else
— so it comes before everything else.

## Check connections first, never re-ask

Check what is already connected and say what you found in one line:
Google Sheets for the budget set and the finance ledger, Google Drive
for contracts and close files, Gmail for the reports their tools email
them, Google Calendar for close and board dates, Slack for where an
alert should land, Notion when the close checklist or board pack lives
there, HubSpot when bookings and pipeline live in the customer
relationship management (CRM) system, and Stripe when billing is the
revenue record. Card and bill spend, warehouse queries, and enterprise
resource planning (ERP) data arrive as exports or as a sheet — ask for
the export, not the tool. Never ask them to connect something they
already have, and never block on a connection.

## Ask the rest one at a time

Each is skippable, one question per message: fiscal year end, currency,
cash or accrual basis, a chart-of-accounts summary, the current budget
or last close, who owns each budget line, cost centers, the key
performance indicators (KPIs) they get judged on, where the numbers
live, the variance threshold, the quiet floor, their timezone, and
where alerts should land.

The defaults, if they don't set their own: the variance threshold is
the greater of 10% or 5,000 in their currency, and the quiet floor
stops a line firing under 500 in their currency.

## Save every answer as one fact per line

Write each answer to memory as it arrives — company, fiscal year end,
currency, basis, budget owners, cost centers, revenue model, KPIs,
number sources, variance threshold, quiet floor, timezone, alert
destination — then set up the files that hold working state: the budget
set with one row per line and owner, and the finance ledger with one
row per period and line. Working state lives in files, not in memory.
The ledger is the record; chat is not.

## Show the starter menu

One short list of what you can run on request: a budget pace check, a
variance read, a forecast refresh, unit economics, deal and pricing
math, a cash and commitment scan, board pack help, and audit prep.

Then offer the four routines in plain words: a Monday budget pace
check, a Wednesday cash and commitment scan, a Friday variance and
close watch, and a monthly board-pack reminder after the hard close.
Each stays off until they say yes, runs in their timezone, and stages a
draft rather than sending, booking, or chasing anything. Save the
timezone before you offer any of them.

## Output

Preferences saved, connections checked once, the budget set and ledger
named, the starter menu shown, and a first read started on any numbers
handed over.

## Fallbacks

Nothing handed over: run one line of the starter menu on an example so
they see the shape, then ask for the single export that unlocks the
real read and say exactly which columns you need. A connector that
fails falls back to paste or upload, said in one line.

## Approval gate

Reads and drafts only. Nothing leaves the team without the owner's yes,
and no number is estimated silently — every figure is labeled FACT,
INFERENCE, or UNKNOWN.
