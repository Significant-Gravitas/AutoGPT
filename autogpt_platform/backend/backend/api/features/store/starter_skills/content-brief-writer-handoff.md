# Content brief writer handoff

Use this when someone picks a topic, a calendar row or a URL and needs a brief
another person can build from. The brief is the deliverable — do not write the
finished piece unless they ask for it outright.

A page meant to rank in search takes its intent read from the search engine
results, and that read belongs in the SEO content brief. Run that one first
where it applies, then use this to turn it into something a writer can be
handed.

## Confirm the target in one line

Name the asset, the primary term or hook, who it is for, and where it ships.
Ask only what the calendar row does not already say. No calendar row means you
build from the topic alone and say which parts are your read rather than their
research.

## Read what already exists

Read the pages or posts already doing this job well. Note what all of them
cover, and — more usefully — what none of them do. That gap is usually the
angle.

## Write the brief

- **Angle.** One or two sentences on the take that makes this worth reading.
- **Reader and moment.** Who lands here and what they need next.
- **Hook and supporting points.** The primary term or hook, the supporting
  points, and the questions the asset has to answer outright.
- **Outline.** Sections with one line under each on what belongs there.
- **Proof.** The data, examples, screenshots or customer lines the writer needs
  — and the named person to get each one from. A claim with no source becomes
  `[need this: what]`, never a filled-in fact.
- **Links in and out**, with the actual URLs.
- **Titles.** Two title options plus a draft meta title and meta description,
  and one line on the single thing that would make this the best of its kind.
- **Product fit.** Where the product gets mentioned, and the one call to action
  (CTA) the asset carries — or none, when it earns none.
- **Practicals.** Deadline, reviewer, and who approves, taken from the calendar
  row or asked for once.

## Add the voice line

Close with the two or three voice rules that bite hardest on this specific
asset, quoted from the voice guide. No voice guide yet means you brief against
house style and say so.

## Where it goes

Hand the brief back in chat, save it dated, and send it to the destination they
picked — a document, a workspace page, or an item on their backlog. Then mark
the calendar row briefed.

## What not to do

Never invent a statistic, a customer name, a quote or a date to make the brief
look complete. Never mark the row drafted, approved or scheduled off your own
say-so; those move on something real. Never promise a ranking or a timeline.
