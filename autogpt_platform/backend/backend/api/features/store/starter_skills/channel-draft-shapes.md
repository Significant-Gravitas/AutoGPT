# Channel draft shapes

Use this when someone hands over notes rather than a draft, or when copy needs
the shape its channel rewards.

Two neighbouring jobs go elsewhere: cutting one asset into several posts across
platforms is repurposing, and a whole lifecycle email program is a sequence
map. This skill writes one piece, well.

## List what you have before you write a word

Write down the facts you actually hold and mark what is missing. A missing
price, metric, date or customer name becomes `[need this: what]` and stays
visible in the draft. An empty slot is a question for the owner, not a writing
problem.

## Say who reads it and what they do next

One line. Everything else in the draft serves that line.

## Name the three voice rules that bite hardest

Check the voice guide and name the three rules that matter most for this
specific piece. Then write one version, not three.

## Hold the shape

- **Email** — a subject under 50 characters in sentence case, a preheader of 40
  to 130 characters that earns the open, a first line that does real work, 50 to
  125 words across three to six sentences, one ask on its own line, no signature
  theater.
- **Landing page** — a headline naming the outcome, a subhead saying how, three
  proof blocks each carrying a specific number or example, one call to action
  (CTA) repeated at the top and the bottom, and an FAQ only where there are real
  objections.
- **Post** — one hook line, three to eight short lines, one concrete detail
  nobody else could have written, no hashtag stack. A thread gets a first post
  that stands alone, then three to six posts of one point each.
- **Blog** — the promise inside the first two sentences, sections that stand
  alone, an example wherever an adjective wants to go, one CTA that fits the
  funnel stage. A post built to rank gets its brief first.
- **Direct message** — two to four sentences, one ask phrased as the next step
  with its link or reply cue, no preamble.
- **Release notes** — grouped by what the reader can now do, one line each,
  plain verbs, no ticket numbers in the body.

No channel named means you ask once. Do not guess the channel — the shape is
most of the work.

## Tighten, then hand it back

Cut hype, filler openers, and any claim with no number or source behind it.
Then hand back three things in this order: the draft, the gaps you marked, and
two alternative openers.

## What not to do

Never invent a customer, a metric, a quote, a price, a launch date or a
testimonial to fill a hole. Never publish, post, send or schedule without a yes
on that specific piece.
