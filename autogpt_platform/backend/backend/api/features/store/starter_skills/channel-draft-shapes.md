# Channel draft shapes

Use this when notes, bullets or a transcript need to become one piece shaped
for the place it will be read. Shape does much of the persuading, so settle
it before polishing sentences. A piece meant to rank gets a content brief
first.

## Settle the channel and the job

No channel named: ask once. If they shrug, pick the likeliest and say why at
the top. Then write one sentence naming who opens this and what they do next
("Ops leads on the free plan; book a setup call"). Every paragraph serves it.

## Inventory the facts, flag the holes

Check the facts you hold against what the shape needs. Each missing one goes
in as `[need this: what]` where it belongs ("Plans start at [need this: Team
plan price]") and stays visible; never blur a sentence to hide it. A
customer's words from a transcript are a quote only with their permission.
Otherwise paraphrase, drop the name, and log permission as a gap.

## Pick three voice rules

From the voice guide, choose the three rules this piece is likeliest to break
(release notes drift into jargon, landing pages into superlatives). From the
messaging matrix, note the proof points and the claims never made. Write one
draft, not a menu of versions.

## The shapes

- **Email.** Subject at most 50 characters, sentence case, saying what is
  inside; a 40 to 130 character preheader that adds to it. Open with the news,
  not a greeting. 50 to 125 words, three to six sentences, a single request
  set apart on its own line. Sign with a name, not a banner of titles.
- **Landing page.** Headline states the result; subhead says how. Three proof
  sections, each resting on a real number, named example or screenshot. One
  call to action (CTA), same wording near the top and at the end. FAQ only for
  objections buyers have actually raised.
- **Social post.** A first line that stops the scroll alone, then three to
  eight short lines with at least one detail only this team could know. Two
  hashtags at most. A thread opens with a post that works solo, then three to
  six posts making a single point apiece.
- **Blog post.** The payoff is clear by sentence two. Each section survives a
  reader who jumped straight to it. Swap every "powerful" or "seamless" for
  the example that proves it. One CTA fitted to the funnel stage.
- **Direct message.** Two to four sentences: why this person, then one next
  step they can take in one move. No warm-up.
- **Release notes.** Sorted by what users can newly do, one line per change
  led by a plain verb. No ticket numbers or code names; invisible changes like
  refactors stay out.
- **Anything else** (a webinar invite, a one-pager): borrow the closest shape
  and say which.

## Tighten

Strike hype and throat-clearing, cut claims lacking a number or source, check
against the never-say list and the shape's limits, and confirm every marker
survived.

## Hard lines

Do not fabricate customers, metrics, quotes, prices, launch dates or
testimonials to make a draft look done. Nothing is published, posted, sent or
scheduled without a yes on that exact version.

## What you hand back

The reader-and-action sentence, the draft, the gap list (each marker with who
likely holds the answer), and two alternative openers from a different angle,
not a reword. Save the draft dated.

## Fallbacks

- No voice guide: use plain house style and say so.
- Too thin for the shape: return the skeleton with markers and name the two or
  three facts that would unlock it.
