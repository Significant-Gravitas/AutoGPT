# VIP and white-glove care

Use this when a premium-tier, enterprise, or exec-flagged customer needs
help. Tier the case, own it personally, and keep stakeholders warm until
resolved.

## What you need

The case, the customer's tier and history, the VIP tiers and exec path from
prefs, and the SLA cadence for the tier.

## Run white-glove care

1. Tier the case on arrival: confirm the tier from the record, not the tone.
   A VIP down is P1; a VIP ask with a path keeps its priority but gets the
   white-glove handling below. An untiered customer asking loudly is not a
   VIP — say so plainly.
2. Own it by name: one owner, instant ack with a named human and a
   first-update time, and updates on the tier's cadence even when the news is
   "still working". Silence is the failure mode.
3. Work the issue with the matching skill (Enterprise identity SSO support,
   Service recovery and goodwill, Fraud and chargeback defense), but keep the
   VIP thread separate from the work thread: the customer sees plain
   progress, never internal thrash. Draft every update before it goes out.
4. For exec-flagged cases, draft the stakeholder line too: impact, what is
   being done, next update time. Exec updates go out only after owner yes,
   and carry the SLA clock across every handoff. Every handoff writes an
   internal note and opens with "I've read the conversation so far" so the
   customer never re-explains.
5. Close white-glove: confirm the fix with the customer, summarize what
   changed, and log tier, cadence kept, and lesson. Offer a proactive
   check-in draft for a week out.

## Output

The tiered case, the cadence plan, staged customer and stakeholder updates,
and the close-out log.

## Fallbacks

No tier list in prefs means you ask for the tiers once, treat exec-flagged
as VIP meanwhile, and mark tier INFERENCE until confirmed.
