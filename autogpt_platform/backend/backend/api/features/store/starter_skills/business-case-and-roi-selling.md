# Business case and ROI selling

Use this when a deal needs the money case. Start from the deal sheet with
qualification state, the buyer's stated metrics and pains with quotes, what
the user sells and at what price band, and the proof they are allowed to cite.

## Build the case from buyer words only

The problem and its cost in their quotes, the measurable outcome they stated,
and the consequence if nothing changes. A metric the buyer never said stays
out — our description of their problem does not count.

## Plan the demo from the case

Three buyer outcomes, the click-path per outcome, the proof line per outcome,
and the trap questions to avoid — not a feature tour. Write per-stakeholder
variants: business case for the CFO, workflow for end users, risk mitigation
for legal and procurement. Score proof-of-concept trials with entry and exit
criteria, a dated owner each side, and what a pass earns.

## Model the ROI with cited numbers only

Current-state cost, future-state cost, the delta, payback period, total cost
of ownership, and net present value, plus a risks and mitigation box. Every
input is FACT with a source or it stays out; assumptions get their own box
with the reason stated. Never invent a number.

## Draft the proposal from the case

Scope, price band, terms, and the one ask with a date. Price and terms stay
inside the approved ladder bands — anything beyond is flagged, never promised.
Every concession names its paired buyer give (term, timing, scope), sequenced
easy to hard with diminishing size, and the walk-away is stated before any
external draft; one-time discounts are confirmed in writing to protect the
renewal anchor, and price realization (actual over list) is tracked. Counter
procurement with the champion-armed ROI case and legal redlines with a
deadline that holds implementation resources. Before any send: pricing
verified, delivery timeline confirmed, similar-use-case proof attached,
proofread — then owner yes. After every verbal yes, send a written recap memo
(final terms, deliverables, milestones, contacts) and log each concession in
the CRM with give, get, approver, and rationale.

## What you hand back

The ROI business case with sourced numbers, the demo or proof-of-concept plan,
and the proposal draft, saved to file. Show them in chat, take one round of
edits, then save.

## Fallbacks

No buyer-stated metric means you name the gap and write the one question the
next call must answer before the case goes anywhere. No approved price band
means scope-only drafts until they give it.

## Approval gate

The proposal, statement of work, and any priced page ship only with your
explicit yes.
